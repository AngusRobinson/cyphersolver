fifty-six ciphers, one report

Contributed research · October 2026

George Lasry’s BnF cipher research, October 2026

Fifty-six enciphered letters and dispatches in the Bibliothèque nationale de France, from Tranchedini in 1452 to Napoleon III in 1859, deciphered by George Lasry with ChatGPT Astra 6 and Anthropic Opus 5.5

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 13 October 2026 · report dated 10 October 2026 · spot-checked and posted here 13 October 2026

On this pageThe reportOur checkCiphertext-onlyDeciphered using a matching keyPlaintext availableTwo excerptsSources
These results are George Lasry’s. He transcribed and deciphered the fifty-six items below, working with ChatGPT Astra 6 and Anthropic Opus 5.5, and reported them in BnF cipher research (10 October 2026), which he sent to Daniel Bourdeau on 13 October 2026. This project did not repeat his work. It checked one sample of each item against the Gallica image and filed his result, with that check, in a folder of the repository. The check is a sample, not a second solution.

01 The report

George Lasry went through ciphered material in the BnF’s Français, Dupuy, Clairambault, Italien, Portugais and nouvelles acquisitions series on Gallica. For each item his report gives the shelfmark and folios, the correspondents and date, the size of the key, how many cipher elements were transcribed and how many identified, the source of the key or plaintext when there was one, and an excerpt of the decipherment with an English translation. The report has three sections:

  • Ciphertext-only (13 items): the key was recovered from the ciphertext alone.
  • Deciphered using a matching key (3 items): a surviving key, in the BnF or in print, was found to fit.
  • Plaintext available (40 items): a contemporary decipherment, between the lines or on another folio, or a clear copy, gave the plaintext, and the key was rebuilt from it.

One item, Desiderio l’Abbé to Nevers, 1577 (Français 3198), had been deciphered here a few days earlier from AngusRobinson’s key (pull request #20, 7 October 2026). Lasry’s independent solution agrees and is recorded on that page. The other fifty-five have their own folders in the repository, each with his metadata, his excerpts and translations, the Gallica links and our spot-check.

02 Our check

For each item we opened the Gallica image he cited (or found the right one when the link was missing or pointed at the wrong canvas), checked that the folio, the cipher and the clear words matched his description, and tested one sample. Where his key table was in the report we decoded a short run of cipher with it; where a contemporary decipherment is written between the lines we compared it with his excerpt. Items were marked confirmed when the sample agreed, and partly confirmed when the folio and cipher matched but the sample could not be decoded at the available resolution, or the reading is partial in his own figures.

  • Batch A (28 items): 11 confirmed, 17 partly confirmed.
  • Batch B (28 items): 17 confirmed (6 decoded by us with his key, 11 checked against the contemporary interlinear decipherment), 11 partly confirmed.
  • None contradicted.

The check found a handful of corrections, now in the folders. Two folders were renamed: the Vergier letters are all of 1696 (vergier1696), and the second Du Bellay item, ff. 65–66, names Pope Julius III, so 1550–55 (dubellay65). Several Gallica links were fixed: the Sega cipher is f148v, canvas 158; Sessa is Français 23054, canvas 272; Dampierre is Français 3414 image 261; Berny and La Boderie are Français 3516 images 227 and 103; Le Rouge f32r is image 61; and image 37 of btv1b9060232m is the Birago letter, not its key. The La Boderie and Berny letters are printed in Jeannin’s Négociations (1875), and the Jeannin item probably is. One clear-text line of the Méry de Vic excerpt differs from the manuscript, and Duquesne’s excerpt is fuller than the contemporary interlinear. The Salviati and Fogliani letters name their recipients in the heading.

06 Two excerpts

Filippo Sega to Cardinal Caetano, 9 January 1591 (Français 3613 f148v). The key is no. 65 in Meister’s Die Geheimschrift im Dienste der päpstlichen Kurie (1906); our check decoded the run after “di ordine di” with it.

Italian, Lasry’s reading Che Lignerac è stato inuiato d’ordine di Umena in Poitu et Bretagna a solecitar la impresa di Tours per doue ua anco a questi efeto un religioso acciò se l uno fose impedito l altro pasi.

Lignerac has been sent by order of Umena to Poitou and Brittany to press the undertaking at Tours. A religious man is also going there for this purpose, so that if one were prevented, the other might get through. (Lasry’s translation.)

Herbault to Bullion, 29 July 1625 (Français 3944 f99r). His excerpt agrees word for word with the contemporary decipherment written over the three cipher rows.

French, Lasry’s reading croions que les forces presentes des [Ennemis] et les considerations de M. de Savoye pour la conservation de ses estats nous imposeront une loy assez absolue pour ce regard

We believe that the enemy’s present forces and Monsieur de Savoy’s concern for preserving his states will impose a fairly absolute rule on us in this respect. (Lasry’s translation.)
