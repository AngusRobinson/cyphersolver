GILINO — WORKING DECIPHERMENT AND ITALIAN READING
7 October 2026

The cipher system has been recovered: an Italian syllabary combined with
single-letter signs, presumed nulls, and a small nomenclator. The surviving
transcriptions and some manuscript signs remain ambiguous. This is a
complete working reading of all nine supplied photographs, with gaps and
conjectures exposed; it is not a claim that every word is settled.

Identification: British Library, Cotton MS Vespasian C IV, ff.214r–218v.
The supplied photographs cover the nine faces 214r through 218r; no photograph
identified as 218v is supplied. The clear date is 15 September 1527, Paredes.
Publication metadata update, 14 October 2026: the address leaf documented
by the existing repository edition identifies the recipient as Francesco II
Sforza, Duke of Milan, at Cremona. This supersedes the earlier tentative
identification in the local reading; the cipher text below is unchanged.
Catalogue: https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-001103269

The text is Italian, with occasional Latin, including pro opprimenda Italia.
The subscription is 'Servo el Gilino'; 'Servo' is not a demonstrated forename.
The author's exact first name remains unestablished.

CONTENT
The report discusses the grand chancellor's standing at the imperial court;
intercepted letters and assistance to Genoa; French and English negotiations
over Milan; papal diplomacy; the hostility of some Ferrara/Colonna agents;
the Infante and the state of Milan; money, troops, and the loss of Genoa.
It includes already-clear paragraphs about the French ambassadors, Ludovico
Catto, a sale in the kingdom of Naples, and Marco Pio. Those passages are
identified as cleartext, not counted as cryptanalytic discoveries.

READING CONVENTIONS
The following Italian edition introduces word boundaries and punctuation,
and regularizes u/v when clear. Historical forms are otherwise retained.
[Word?] = editorial conjecture; [literal: ...] or [letterale: ...] preserves
unresolved output. 'Imperatore?' and 'Sua Maestà?' are interpretations of
word codes; their precise original expansions remain unproved.
Square brackets also expand ordinary manuscript abbreviations; surrounding
labels distinguish cleartext from deciphered text. Local notes explain
substantive conjectures. The literal decode and alignment preserve the
underlying evidence, including the readings that resist interpretation.
